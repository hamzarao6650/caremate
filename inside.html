How it works

From a webcam frame to a kind word.

Everything below runs in today's prototype, in a web browser on an ordinary laptop webcam. The first three steps happen on the device.

It sees

A body-pose and face-mesh model (MediaPipe) tracks how the person is sitting and moving.

  • Activity: sitting, standing, walking or lying down
  • Posture against the person's own calibrated baseline
Live today

It reads how they are

An on-device expression model (HSEmotion) reads mood. The camera also measures pulse, explained below.

  • Facial expression
  • Heart rate, compared with their own 7-day range
Live today, pulse experimental

It knows who it is with

Face recognition (ArcFace) picks whose memory to use. Only a 512-number face signature leaves the device, never a photo.

  • One private memory per person
  • Unrecognized visitors get no profile
Live today

It speaks up

The chat uses the readings and the person's memory, and it starts the conversation when something changes.

"You have been leaning forward a while. Sit back for me?"
Live today

How the memory grows

  • After each chat reply, a separate step pulls out what is worth keeping, such as likes, dislikes, hobbies and medical history.
  • The conversation never waits for this step, so replies stay quick.
  • Memories are plain Markdown notes that staff can read, search, correct and export.

What leaves the room

  • The 512-number face signature, for recognition.
  • A few small frames a minute, sent to a cloud vision model for a one-line scene description.
  • Chat messages and memory notes, stored per person.
Pulse from a camera

Every heartbeat changes skin color, just a little.

Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into the skin and shifts its color by a fraction of a percent. CareMate measures that shift from the camera. The technique is called remote photoplethysmography (rPPG).

Step 1

Finds bare skin

The face mesh finds three patches of bare skin, the forehead and both cheeks, and averages thousands of pixels in each frame.

→
Step 2

Turns color into a pulse wave

Every second, about ten seconds of color become a pulse wave using the published POS method. CareMate then tracks the pulse over time so one noisy peak cannot take over.

→
Step 3

Shows it only when clean

It checks motion and light first. If the signal is poor it says "hold still" or "too dark" instead of guessing. Each reading is compared with that person's own 7-day range.

Method: Wang et al., "Algorithmic Principles of Remote PPG," IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, 2017. Thresholds were tuned on real webcam recordings with a finger-clip reference. A wellness signal, not a medical device, and not yet clinically validated.

The 90-day pilot

Test it on the devices you already have.

We plan to offer care homes and home-care agencies a free 90-day pilot on the webcams and tablets they already own, so there is nothing to buy. Here is how we propose it would run.

Before day 1

Agree the baseline and consent

The home shares its current response times, incident counts and staff hours, so results can be compared with its own numbers. Residents, or the people who decide for them, agree before anyone is enrolled.

Weeks 1 and 2

Set up and calibrate

CareMate is set up on the home's existing webcams and tablets. Each resident's posture baseline is calibrated, and shift leads learn to read the daily summary.

Weeks 3 to 12

Run it day to day

CareMate checks in with residents and sends staff short updates and a daily summary, never a video feed. Our team meets shift leads every week to fix what is not working.

Day 90

Compare and decide

We share a before-and-after report against the home's own baseline. The home then decides whether to continue with a paid subscription.

Proposed plan. Pilots begin once the pilot-ready stage ships: the caregiver app and alerts, fall detection and a security review. These are on the roadmap, not in today's prototype.

What we measure

The numbers a Director of Nursing cares about.

Every number is compared with that home's own baseline, not an industry average.

Response times

How long it takes staff to reach a resident after a change is spotted, before and during the pilot.

Incidents

Falls and other reportable incidents during the pilot, compared with the same period before it.

Staff hours

Time spent on routine room checks and charting, to see whether staff get time back for hands-on care.

Buyer

Director of Nursing or Operations

Sponsors the pilot, owns the baseline and makes the final decision.

Champions

Shift leads and caregivers

Use the daily summary and tell us what helps and what gets in the way.

At the center

Residents and families

Asked first. No cameras in bathrooms or other private spaces.

Our side

The CareMate team

Sets up the system, trains staff, checks in every week and writes the final report.
